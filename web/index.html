    🏠

    Built from the real thing

    Buildings from the 3D BAG, every pavement, garden and canal from the Dutch government's large-scale map, aerial photos at 25 cm.

    👋

    Meet people there

    Everyone nearby walks around as themselves. Tap someone to call them; invite more for a group. Their voice comes from where they stand.

    📍

    Send a meeting link

    Share where you're standing. Your friends open the link and arrive right beside you, a few steps apart.

    ⚡

    No install, no account

    It runs in the browser on a laptop or phone. Calls are encrypted from browser to browser; nothing in between can listen in.

    First time? It's easy.

    There's nothing to learn before you start walking.

    Pick a place

    You start on a real street. Type any address in the ☰ menu to jump there.

    Walk around

    Use WASD or just click where you want to go. Drag to look. On a phone, use the stick.

    Look for others

    Real people have a name over their head. Tap one to talk. Everyone else on the pavement is a passer-by.

    Where it all comes from

    No artist drew these streets. Every piece comes from open data that anyone can download.

    Buildings

    3D BAG · TU Delft

    Every building in the Netherlands as a 3D model with its real height and roof shape, made from the national building register and laser height scans. 3dbag.nl

    Pavements, gardens, water

    BGT · Kadaster, via PDOK

    The government's large-scale map: every road, pavement, hedge, lawn and canal is drawn to within tens of centimetres. pdok.nl

    Addresses and aerial photos

    BAG and Luchtfoto · PDOK

    Every front door has its real house number. The ground and roofs are coloured by aerial photos at 25 cm per pixel.

    Shops, street names, roads

    OpenStreetMap contributors

    The names of shops, bus stops and streets, and the road network that cars and the route line follow. openstreetmap.org

    Birds on the water

    Sovon Vogelonderzoek Nederland

    Which swans, geese and ducks you meet on a pond follows the national bird survey. See the numbers →
